2Sine and cosineTheorem 3.3.1 · 1/2
Theorem 3.3.1Derivative of the sine functionp. 215
$$\frac{d}{dx}(\sin x)=\cos x$$
Proof
  1. From the limit definition of the derivative, we have$$\frac{d}{dx}(\sin x)=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{\sin(x+h)-\sin x}{h}$$
  2. Using the identity $\;\sin(a+b)=\sin a\cos b+\cos a\sin b$, we have$$\frac{d}{dx}(\sin x)=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{\sin x\cos(h)+\cos x\sin(h)-\sin x}{h}$$$$=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{\sin x\cos(h)-\sin x+\cos x\sin(h)}{h}$$$$=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{\sin x\big(\cos(h)-1\big)+\cos x\sin(h)}{h}$$

2Sine and cosineTheorem 3.3.1 · 2/2
Theorem 3.3.1Derivative of the sine functionp. 215
$$\frac{d}{dx}(\sin x)=\cos x$$
Proof (continued)
  1. $$=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{\sin x\big(\cos(h)-1\big)}{h}+\lim_{h\to0}\frac{\cos x\sin(h)}{h}$$$$=\sin x\lim_{h\to0}\frac{\cos(h)-1}{h}+\cos x\lim_{h\to0}\frac{\sin(h)}{h}$$
  2. Recall from Theorem 2.2.3 that$$\lim_{h\to0}\frac{\cos(h)-1}{h}=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\lim_{h\to0}\frac{\sin(h)}{h}=1$$
  3. Thus,$$\frac{d}{dx}(\sin x)=\sin x(0)+\cos x(1)=\cos x$$

2Sine and cosineExample 1(b)
Example 1(b)p. 216
Quotient ruleFind the derivative of $\;y=\dfrac{1+\sin x}{x+\cos x}$
  1. Using quotient rule, we have$$\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{1+\sin x}{x+\cos x}\right)$$$$=\frac{(x+\cos x)\frac{d}{dx}(1+\sin x)-(1+\sin x)\frac{d}{dx}(x+\cos x)}{(x+\cos x)^{2}}$$$$=\frac{(x+\cos x)\cos x-(1+\sin x)(1-\sin x)}{(x+\cos x)^{2}}=\frac{x\cos x+\cos^{2}x-\left(1-\sin^{2}x\right)}{(x+\cos x)^{2}}$$$$=\frac{x\cos x+\cos^{2}x-\cos^{2}x}{(x+\cos x)^{2}}=\frac{x\cos x}{(x+\cos x)^{2}}$$
Answer $y'=\dfrac{x\cos x}{(x+\cos x)^{2}}$

3tan, sec, csc and cotTheorem 3.3.3
Theorem 3.3.3Derivative of the tangent functionp. 217
$$\frac{d}{dx}(\tan x)=\sec^{2}x$$
Proof
  1. Using quotient rule, we have$$\frac{d}{dx}(\tan x)=\frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{\sin x}{\cos x}\right)$$$$=\frac{\cos x\frac{d}{dx}(\sin x)-\sin x\frac{d}{dx}(\cos x)}{\cos^{2}x}$$$$=\frac{\cos x(\cos x)-\sin x(-\sin x)}{\cos^{2}x}=\frac{\cos^{2}x+\sin^{2}x}{\cos^{2}x}$$$$=\frac{1}{\cos^{2}x}=\left(\frac{1}{\cos x}\right)^{2}=\sec^{2}x$$

Theorem 3.3.4Derivatives of secant, cosecant and cotangentp. 217
$$\text{a. }\ \frac{d}{dx}(\sec x)=\sec x\tan x\qquad\text{b. }\ \frac{d}{dx}(\csc x)=-\csc x\cot x\qquad\text{c. }\ \frac{d}{dx}(\cot x)=-\csc^{2}x$$

Theorem 3.3.4 (a)Proofp. 217
$$\frac{d}{dx}(\sec x)=\sec x\tan x$$
Proof
  1. Since $\;\sec x=\dfrac{1}{\cos x}$, using quotient rule, we have$$\frac{d}{dx}(\sec x)=\frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{1}{\cos x}\right)$$$$=\frac{\cos x\frac{d}{dx}(1)-(1)\frac{d}{dx}(\cos x)}{\cos^{2}x}=\frac{-(-\sin x)}{\cos^{2}x}$$$$=\frac{\sin x}{\cos^{2}x}=\frac{1}{\cos x}\cdot\frac{\sin x}{\cos x}=\sec x\tan x$$

Theorem 3.3.4 (c)Proofp. 218
$$\frac{d}{dx}(\cot x)=-\csc^{2}x$$
Proof
  1. Since $\;\cot x=\dfrac{\cos x}{\sin x}$, using quotient rule, we have$$\frac{d}{dx}(\cot x)=\frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{\cos x}{\sin x}\right)$$$$=\frac{\sin x\frac{d}{dx}(\cos x)-\cos x\frac{d}{dx}(\sin x)}{\sin^{2}x}$$$$=\frac{\sin x(-\sin x)-\cos x(\cos x)}{\sin^{2}x}=\frac{-\sin^{2}x-\cos^{2}x}{\sin^{2}x}$$$$=\frac{-\left(\sin^{2}x+\cos^{2}x\right)}{\sin^{2}x}=\frac{-1}{\sin^{2}x}=-\csc^{2}x$$

Related Problem 3p. 219
Slope of the tangent lineFind the slope of the tangent line to the graph of $\;y=\csc x-2\cos x\;$ at $\;x=\dfrac{\pi}{6}$.
  1. $$\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{d}{dx}(\csc x)-2\frac{d}{dx}(\cos x)=-\csc x\cot x+2\sin x$$
  2. The slope of the tangent line to the graph of $y$ at $x=\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ is:$$m=\left.\frac{dy}{dx}\right|_{x=\frac{\pi}{6}}=-\csc\frac{\pi}{6}\cot\frac{\pi}{6}+2\sin\frac{\pi}{6}=-(2)\left(\sqrt3\right)+2\left(\frac12\right)=-2\sqrt3+1$$
Answer $m=-2\sqrt3+1$

5Challenging and CommunicateChallenging Problem 1
Challenging Problem 1p. 223
A shifted cosineGiven that $f(x)=\cos(x+h)$, where $h$ is constant, show that $f'(x)=-\sin(x+h)$.
  1. $$f'(x)=\lim_{t\to0}\frac{f(x+t)-f(x)}{t}=\lim_{t\to0}\frac{\cos[(x+t)+h]-\cos(x+h)}{t}$$$$=\lim_{t\to0}\frac{\cos(x+h)\cos t-\sin(x+h)\sin t-\cos(x+h)}{t}$$$$=\cos(x+h)\underbrace{\lim_{t\to0}\frac{\cos t-1}{t}}_{\to\,0}-\sin(x+h)\underbrace{\lim_{t\to0}\frac{\sin t}{t}}_{\to\,1}$$$$=-\sin(x+h)$$
Answer $f'(x)=-\sin(x+h)$
الحل مطابق لصورة الحل المرسلة. الإجابة: $f'(x)=-\sin(x+h)$ — عدد الضغطات: 5.

Why is the derivative of tan x equal to sec² x?Explain why the derivative of $\tan x$ is $\sec^{2}x$. Use the quotient rule to derive it.
  1. $$\frac{d}{dx}(\tan x)=\frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{\sin x}{\cos x}\right)=\frac{\cos x\cos x-\sin x(-\sin x)}{(\cos x)^{2}}=\frac{\sin^{2}x+\cos^{2}x}{(\cos x)^{2}}$$$$=\frac{1}{(\cos x)^{2}}=\sec^{2}x$$
Answer $\dfrac{d}{dx}(\tan x)=\sec^{2}x$
